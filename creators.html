The artists behind the stories

Komik Creators

Meet some of the storytellers and artists whose imagination helped shape Filipino komiks.

1910–1997

  • Artist

Tony Velasquez

Tony Velasquez is widely recognized as the father of Tagalog comics. In 1928, he and writer Romualdo Ramos introduced Kenkoy, a comic-strip character whose humorous everyday adventures helped popularize comics in the Philippines. His work helped establish the comic strip as a familiar form of Filipino entertainment.

1916–1988

  • Writer

Mars Ravelo

Mars Ravelo was one of the most influential writers in Philippine komiks. He created iconic characters such as Darna, Dyesebel, Captain Barbell, and Lastikman. His stories combined fantasy, adventure, romance, and Filipino cultural themes, and many of his creations were later adapted into films and television shows.

1919–1998

  • Artist
  • Writer

Francisco V. Coching

Francisco V. Coching was a prominent Filipino comic artist and writer. He is best known for his works Hagibis and Lapu-Lapu and his contributions to the development of Philippine komiks. Coching was known for his detailed artwork, dynamic characters, and cinematic storytelling. He helped elevate the artistic quality of Philippine komiks.

1925–2000

  • Artist

Alfredo Alcala

An internationally recognized Filipino comic book illustrator celebrated for his intricate pen-and-ink drawings. He worked for major American comic publishers and helped introduce Filipino artistic talent to international audiences. His works include Voltar, Conan the Barbarian, and Swamp Thing.

1926–2002

  • Artist

Larry Alcala

Larry Alcala became famous for depicting Filipino humor, habits, and everyday life. His long-running works include Kalabog en Bosyo and Slice of Life, the latter known for its crowded illustrations in which readers search for a hidden self-portrait of the artist. He was posthumously named a National Artist for Visual Arts in 2018.

1928–1995

  • Artist

Nestor Redondo

An influential Filipino illustrator known for his detailed and expressive artwork. Known for works such as Darna, Swamp Thing, and Rima the Jungle Girl. He worked with international comic publishers, including DC Comics, and became one of the prominent Filipino artists in American comics.

1929–2010

  • Writer

Pablo S. Gomez

A prolific Filipino komiks writer known for creating engaging stories involving romance, family relationships, comedy, and social issues. His works like Mga Anak ni Facifica Falayfay also inspired adaptations for television and film.

1932–2012

  • Artist

Tony DeZuniga

A Filipino comic artist who made significant contributions to the American comic book industry. He worked on Jonah Hex and Black Orchid. He was among the first Filipino artists recruited by DC Comics and helped introduce more Filipino illustrators to international publishers.

1968–2019

  • Artist
  • Writer

Gerry Alanguilan

A comic book writer, illustrator, and advocate for Filipino comics. His graphic novel Elmer explores discrimination, humanity, and social prejudice through a world where chickens develop human intelligence. He also founded the Komikero Komiks Museum.
